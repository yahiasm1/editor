# Research

Any subject works: the thing the creator names, the way they say it. Research is there to make the transcript true
and deep, not to choose the subject for them. When the creator asks you to choose, propose three subjects with one
line each and let them pick.

## What to find

- **How it really works**, at expert depth: the mechanism, step by step, with its real terms and real numbers. Go to
  primary sources: the paper, the patent, the engineer's own words, the maker's documentation, the teardown, the
  repository, the regulator. A second-hand article is a lead, not a source.
- **The viewer's wrong picture**, in five words ("the screen feels me press", "the keys are the size I see"). The
  transcript is built to break it.
- **The moment the viewer lived**: the place where the mechanism touches their day (gloves in winter, a wet screen,
  a typo that fixed itself).
- **The second layer**: the case where it fails, or the trick on top, which proves the mechanism.
- **Something to save or send**: a check they can run, a setting, a tool, a fix, or an understanding deep enough to
  explain to a friend.
- **The real material for the pictures**: product photos, teardowns, sections, the real interface, real footage.
  Note the links; the plan fetches them.

## facts.md

Every claim the transcript will make, one row each, checked today:

```
| claim | evidence (a verbatim quote) | URL |
```

Keep scope words ("up to", "in one study", "on supported iPhones"). When something cannot be confirmed, say so and
leave it out of the script. Never invent a number, a tool or a capability. Default year: the current one.

In a workspace project, save the findings as `research.md` and the table as `facts.md` in the project folder.
