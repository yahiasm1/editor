Why the Corner Three Is the Shortest and Best Shot in the NBA

The NBA three-point line is 23 feet 9 inches from the basket at the top of the arc, but only 22 feet in the corners. The court is 50 feet wide with the basket in the middle, and the rulebook keeps the line 3 feet inside each sideline, so in the corner it runs straight and sits 21 inches closer.

The shorter shot goes in more. Last season NBA teams made 38.7% of their corner threes and about 35% of the threes above the break.

In points per shot, a corner three is worth about 1.16. A long two, from 16 feet out to the line, went in 40.1% of the time, which is about 0.80. The corner three is worth about 45% more.

That is why the long two disappeared: 23.6% of every shot in 2004-05, 4.9% in 2025-26.
