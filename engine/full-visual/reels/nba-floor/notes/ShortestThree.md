# ShortestThree (scene `close`, 68.24-74.62 s)

"So the line really isn't the same distance everywhere, and the smartest teams spend every possession trying to reach
the shortest part of it."

What gives a fake away: a "22 ft" mark at the baseline end (it is 22.6 there) instead of rim level; logos on white
tiles; a reading that jumps instead of rolling with the blade; team numbers that do not match BBR; a ball that
teleports between passes.

## The real thing, and where each part comes from

1. The same half court (Rule 1, plan coordinates, PointsPerShotFloor copied), ARC FRAME, the line painted white.
2. 1,000 attempts in their 2025-26 places (League Average shares), neutral white dots, thinning to texture; the
   corner-strip dots brighten again when the teams arrive.
3. The Lufkin tape on the pin at the middle of the basket: pays out to the top (23' 9"), swings to the corner at rim
   level (22' 0"), sweeps the whole line (its reading rolls 22' 0" -> 23' 9" -> 22' 0"), the blade's gold under-glow
   painting the line it passes.
4. The four real logos on the apron under the corners, two each, their corner share counting up under them.
5. The ball from `corner`: top of the key, swing to the left wing and corner, skip pass across to the right corner.
6. The point at rim level on the right corner line: a white cross, "22' 0"", "the shortest three".

## Sound-off test

The tape reads 23' 9" at the top and 22' 0" in the corner, then sweeps and the number rises and falls: the line is
not one distance. Four real team logos land in the corners with their share of shots from there. The ball goes to
the corners, and the closest point on the floor lights: 22' 0", the shortest three.
