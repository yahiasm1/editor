Shaquille O'Neal was so unstoppable that teams started fouling him on purpose. Sounds backwards, right? A foul usually hands the other team free points. But Shaq was the exception, because near the rim he was a seven foot one wrecking ball, and at the free throw line he was a coin flip. Over his career he made just 52.7 percent, and missed 5,317 free throws.

So picture it. Shaq catches the ball under the basket, and before he can even turn, somebody grabs him. Now he's standing alone at the line, fifteen feet out, the whole arena watching. Make one of two, and that's one point instead of an easy dunk.

That trick got a name: Hack-a-Shaq. Dallas coach Don Nelson made it famous in 1999, and Shaq called him a clown. So at their next game, Nelson showed up wearing a clown nose.

And some nights it really worked. On December 8, 2000, Shaq went zero for eleven at the line against Seattle, still an NBA record. The Lakers hired a free throw coach just for him. Hall of Famer Rick Barry offered to teach him his underhand granny shot, and Shaq said he'd shoot zero percent before he'd shoot underhanded.

Even Gregg Popovich joked about it, fouling him five seconds into a season opener and flashing two thumbs up. Years later the league changed the rules, so grabbing a guy away from the ball late in any quarter now gives his team a free throw and the ball.

And Shaq? He still won four championships and three Finals MVPs. So yes, teams fouled him on purpose, because it was the only way to slow him down. Follow for the next wild NBA story, and send this to the friend who can't hit a free throw either.
