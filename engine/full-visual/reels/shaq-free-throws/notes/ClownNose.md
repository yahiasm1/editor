# ClownNose (`clown`, 36.17-46.43 s)

"That trick got a name: Hack-a-Shaq. Dallas coach Don Nelson made it famous in 1999, and Shaq called him a clown.
So at their next game, Nelson showed up wearing a clown nose."

## What I looked at

- `photos/commons-don-nelson-2012-seanpanderson-ccby2.jpg` (725 x 1024, Sean P. Anderson, CC BY 2.0): Nelson in his
  70s, grinning hard, eyes squeezed nearly shut, deep crow's feet and forehead lines, white hair blown up and back,
  dark charcoal jacket, open-collar light grey striped shirt. Under a steel and glass roof (dark girders across a
  pale sky). Hard sun from the viewer's RIGHT: the right cheek and the nose's right flank are lit almost white, the
  left side of the face is in warm shade. Read off a 3x gridded crop: the nose tip (the round ball of the nose) is
  centred at x 320, y 333; nostril wings span x 272-368; the tip ball is about 50 px across. So a real foam nose
  (about 5 cm, a bit wider than his nose tip) is about 105 px across in image pixels. The cast shadow of anything on
  his nose falls to the viewer's left and a little down.
- `photos/commons-clown-nose-robertcudmore-ccbysa2.jpg` (Robert Cudmore, CC BY-SA 2.0): a foam clown nose on a
  sheet. Not a glossy ball: open-cell foam, matte, a fine pore texture over the whole surface, slightly flattened
  sphere (wider than tall, a pressed side), the hole on its face about a quarter of the diameter, a pinched crease on
  top where the foam was cut, a pale dusty scuff. Colour: an orange red, lighter where the light hits (pores catch
  white), saturated in the turn, a soft contact shadow under it. The plan asks for it in foul red #E5383B: the joke
  lands on the reel's foul colour; the pores, the flattening, the hole and the crease are what make it this object.
- `photos/commons-shaq-lakers-1999-lipofsky-ccby3.jpg` (199 x 300, Steve Lipofsky, CC BY 3.0, 20 Dec 1999): Shaq at
  the line in Lakers gold #34, purple trim, ball overhead in both hands, yellow wristband, looking up at the rim,
  dark arena behind. Tiny: shown at most ~290 px wide.
- `logos/nba-mavericks-primary.svg` (cdn.nba.com): today's horse head and ball, royal blue #0064B1, navy #061922,
  silver #BBC4CA, white. Bare on navy. The 1999 team wore the hat logo (not on cdn.nba.com), so the horse names the
  team on "Dallas coach" and is gone before "1999".
- A rubber stamp: Commons search found real ones (File:Stempel_01_KMJ.jpg, File:Stamp,_name_(AM_1965.78.558-5).jpg:
  "turned wooden handle; octagonal ...") but every download returned 429 (three reels share the limit). Drawn from
  what those pages describe and what an office stamp is: a turned hardwood knob (ball top, a waisted neck, a collar),
  lacquered, on a rectangular wooden mount with a paper index label on top, a thin grey foam cushion, and the rubber
  die underneath (letters raised and mirrored). The impression: ink heavy at the letter edges, starved speckle inside
  the strokes, a frame rule around the words, a slight tilt, a few spatter dots. The pad: a pressed tin case with an
  open hinged lid and a felt pad soaked in white pigment ink.

## Think

- **Real things:** the stamp and its pad (redrawn, above), the real Mavericks primary, Nelson's real photo, the real
  1999 Lipofsky photo, the foam nose redrawn from Cudmore's photo, the nose placed at the nose point read off the
  photo (320, 333 of 725 x 1024).
- **Details that make it real:** the stamp dips into the pad before it lands (wet ink sheen on the die); the handle
  squashes a few pixels and the card jolts on contact; the impression is not clean type (speckle, rough edge, frame
  rule, 4 degree tilt), spatter dots; credits inside both photo cards; the nose's pores, flattening, crease, its hole
  turning away as it falls (the hole goes on the wearer's nose); the cast shadow on his cheek to the left, where the
  sun throws it; the squash and the two small bounces; his card shaking with laughter.
- **Word, then change:**
  - frame 0 (That trick got a): the navy card is already there; the stamp rocks above the open ink pad, dips into it.
  - name: lands: the stamp slams on the card, handle squashes, card jolts, spatter.
  - Hack-a-Shaq: lands: the stamp lifts away and HACK-A-SHAQ is left in rough white ink.
  - Dallas: the stamped card slides up and shrinks to a label; the Mavericks logo rises bare.
  - Don: Nelson's card slides in under the logo; the logo shrinks to a corner and fades.
  - Nelson: lands: slow push toward his face.
  - made it famous: a date band rises along the bottom of his card.
  - 1999: ends on: the four digits roll up, one after another, the last landing on the word.
  - and Shaq called him: the small 1999 Lakers card slides in at the left, tilted toward Nelson; on "called" it
    leans in at him.
  - clown (1st): lands: the red foam nose appears high above Nelson's face, swinging on nothing.
  - So at their next game: Shaq's card leaves; Nelson's card centres and grows; the nose keeps hanging over him.
  - Nelson (2nd) showed up: push into his face.
  - wearing a: the nose starts to fall, turning its hole away.
  - clown (2nd): lands: on his nose: squash, two small bounces, shadow on his cheek.
  - nose: ends on: the card shakes with a laugh; hold.
- **Sound-off test:** a name is stamped; it belongs to this coach (Dallas logo, his face, 1999); Shaq faces him from
  his own card; a red nose hangs over the coach and lands on his face. The joke reads without a word.
