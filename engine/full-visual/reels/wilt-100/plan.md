## The viewer's wrong picture

"The greatest scoring night ever must have been a huge event: a packed big-city arena, TV cameras, a highlight
reel everybody has seen." The truth is the opposite at every turn: a half-empty hockey barn in a chocolate town, no
camera, no New York press, one radio tape of one quarter, a photo made up on the spot with a sheet of paper, and a
ball that walked out the door under a kid's arm.

So the reel is about **evidence**: what saw the 100, and what is left of it. Each scene adds or removes one witness.

## The mechanism, at the depth the viewer leaves with

Light on analysis (the creator's rule), but the story has a real chain, and each link is shown working:

1. Where and when: March 2 1962, Hershey Sports Arena, a neutral site (Basketball-Reference
   marks it N; only "Hershey" is said, "neutral site" is never written). Warriors (then of Philadelphia) vs Knicks.
2. Who saw it: 4,124 in the seats (about half), no TV, no New York reporters. What recorded it: radio, and only the
   fourth quarter survives.
3. Why 100 was even possible: he averaged 50.4 that season (80 games, 4,029 points, BBR game log).
4. How it built: 23, 41, 69, then 31 in the fourth (the quarter record; only "23, 41, 69" are said, the fourth is
   shown as the scoreboard reaching 100).
5. How the end got silly: the Knicks fouled other Warriors so the ball never reached him; the Warriors fouled back
   to get the ball again. Real totals in the box: Knicks 32 personal fouls, Warriors 25 (shown as copy, not said).
6. The wild part: a career-bad free throw shooter (.613 that season, BBR) made 28 of 32 (.875) that night.
   The arithmetic of the 100 sits quietly in the box row: 36 field goals x 2 = 72, plus 28 = 100 (seen, not said).
7. The aftermath, each a surviving witness: fans on the floor, final 169-147, the improvised photo, the ball and its
   auction price.

## Once for the reel

- **One world, three scales. The reel travels between them and comes back.**
  - TOWN: Hershey on the real map of Pennsylvania between Philadelphia and New York; Chocolate Avenue with its
    Kiss-shaped streetlights and the HERSHEY smokestacks of the chocolate factory. Where it happened, far from the
    cameras.
  - ARENA: Hershey Sports Arena, redrawn from the real photos: the long barrel-vault concrete roof with its ribs, seats
    rising on all four sides of the long oval floor (steepest along the long sides), a 1962 basketball floor laid in the middle (no three point line, the 12 ft
    keyhole lane of the time), a hanging scoreboard. Where it was seen.
  - HAND: the paper of the night, held close: the ticket, the box score sheet, the free throw tally, the "100" sheet
    of paper, the photo print, the ball, the auction paddle. What is left of it.
- **Thread object: Wilt's points counter**, a 1962 arena flip-number panel (white numerals on black flaps, a
  hairline label "WILT" above, small grey). It is born in the hook at 100, carried down to 0 in `record`, climbs
  through `quarters` (23, 41, 69) docked as the player panel of the arena scoreboard, clicks to 100 in `flood`, and
  in `photo` its printed "100" turns into the handwritten "100" on the sheet of paper (the same numerals, redrawn as
  marker strokes). It is the main picture only in the hook. Everywhere else it is small: hanging under the roof in
  `crowd`, `fouls`, `freethrows`, a panel of the scoreboard in `quarters` and `flood`.
- **The arena scoreboard**, a second recurring real object: a period board hung from the vault (black steel panel,
  white bulb digits, WARRIORS / KNICKS, four round quarter lamps). It appears first in `crowd` (only its lamp 4 is lit:
  the only recording), is the hero of `quarters`, and lands the final in `flood`.
- **The invisible thing, one shape and one colour for the whole reel: witness light, amber #F2B33D** (the gold
  accent of the creator's reels). Everything that saw or kept the 100 glows a small amber point or edge: each
  occupied seat (one dot per spectator), the radio dial and its waves, the flashbulb, the ball under the arm, the
  auction paddle's number. Everything that did NOT see it stays in its real colours or dark grey: the empty seats,
  the dead TV camera, the empty press row. Amber never means anything else; points and scores are white.
- **Era treatment.** The real photos are 1959-1962 black and white: keep them black and white, never tinted, in
  rounded #161B22 cards with the credit top right (22 px grey, "AP photo, public domain"). Drawn period objects
  (ticket, scoresheet, paper) are cream #EFE6D2 paper with real grain, slight curl, soft shadow, ink in near-black
  #1B1B1B, typed in a monospaced typewriter face; real team colours only on team marks.
- **Team marks.** The Warriors of 1962 are the PHILADELPHIA Warriors: their mark on stage is the real jersey
  lettering "PHILA" read off the 100 photo (arched block letters, dark on white). The current cdn.nba.com Warriors
  logo (Bay Bridge) is anachronistic and is never used. The Knicks: the cdn.nba.com Knicks logo, small and bare.
  No period Warriors logo was found on Commons; the PHILA lettering is the mark.
- **Labels:** plain small grey type (#8B949E) with a hairline, 32 px minimum for evidence. Source lines bottom left
  of the stage, 26 px grey: "Basketball-Reference box score", "Basketball-Reference game log 1961-62".
- **No** game footage (none exists, and that is the point), no fake "highlight" frames, no mannequins or
  silhouettes for the 14-year-old, the PR man or the photographer (they are shown by their objects and hands only
  where a real photo exists), no pills or badges, no bar charts, no blue on stage, no dashed lines, no frosted
  panels, no game clock times that are not in the refs, no invented ticket price.
- **Grounds:** all dark, #0D1117 with the house faint grid. The arena interior is the one big warm surface: the
  wooden floor lit by the hanging lights, the concrete vault in cool grey above.
- **The one big move (body):** `flood`, on "poured": the amber witness dots in the seats (the 4,124 counted in
  `crowd`) leave their seats and run down the real aisle gaps of both banks as people, hop the rail and spread over
  the floor around the players, until the boards are covered. Each dot keeps its own path and a small stagger: a
  crowd running, never a liquid. The reel never does anything that loud again.
- **The payoff:** `photo`. The handwritten 100, the flashbulb, and the real photo developing out of white in a tray.
  No hook and no earlier scene shows the 100 photo.
- **Last shot = first shot, resolved:** the hook stands OUTSIDE the arena at night (the real barrel vault, windows
  dark, the counter at 100 above it). `close` returns to that exact frame, then the vault's roof turns see-through
  rib by rib and the few things that saw it glow amber in place, one at a time: the seats, the radio at the press
  table, the flashbulb, the ball. Then the camera pulls up to the map, and the arena is one tiny amber point in a
  dark state.
