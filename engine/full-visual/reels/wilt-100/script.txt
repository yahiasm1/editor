Wilt Chamberlain scored 100 points in one NBA game, and almost nobody saw it. March 2, 1962. Not in some big city arena, but in Hershey, Pennsylvania, the chocolate town, against the New York Knicks. Only 4,124 people showed up, about half the building. There were no TV cameras and no New York reporters, and the only recording is radio audio of the fourth quarter.

So how does a guy score 100? That season Wilt averaged 50.4 points a game, which is still the record. That night he had 23 after one quarter, 41 at the half, and 69 after three. By then the crowd was chanting "Give it to Wilt!"

Then it got silly. The Knicks started fouling the other Warriors just to keep the ball away from him, and the Warriors fouled right back to get it again.

And the wildest part? Wilt was known as a terrible free throw shooter, and that night he made 28 of 32. Other players couldn't believe it. When the 100th point went in, fans poured onto the court, and the Warriors won 169 to 147.

Even the famous photo was an accident. In the locker room, the team's PR man scribbled 100 on a piece of paper, and a photographer who had only come to treat his son snapped Wilt holding it up. And the ball? A 14 year old fan said he walked out with it, and it later sold at auction for 551,844 dollars.
