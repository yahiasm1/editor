# PhotoDeveloping (`photo`, 58.88-67.98 s)

"Even the famous photo was an accident. In the locker room, the team's PR man scribbled 100 on a piece of paper, and
a photographer who had only come to treat his son snapped Wilt holding it up."

## What I looked at (full size)

- `photos/wilt-100-paper-photo-1962-original.jpg` (2276 x 2879, PD). The sheet is plain white typing-size paper held
  by its top right corner (fingertips) and bottom middle (fist), tilted about 13.5 degrees clockwise, the top edge
  slightly wavy, a vertical fold crease running down from under the second 0. The "100": a felt marker with a dry,
  scratchy edge, several passes. The "1" is one leaning stroke with a dot of ink at its foot; the first 0 is an egg
  leaning left, closed with an overlap at the top; the second 0 is rounder, leaning right, heavier on its left side
  (two passes). Stroke about 1/25 of the paper's width. Two small "a" retouch marks on the paper. Behind Wilt: coats
  and jackets hanging on hooks (dark wool overcoats, one lighter jacket with a pale lining band at right), a pale
  wall strip between them, a white locker bench edge at left. Light: one hard flash from camera left (the flash,
  hard shadow edges, the sheet blown almost pure white). Tones: deep blacks in hair, jersey lettering PHILA 13, the
  marker; the paper and jersey the brightest.
- `photos/commons-hersheypark-arena-locker-room-2001-cooper-ccby3.jpg`: the same building's locker room: open wooden
  stalls, a hook rail, wooden bench boxes, pale painted walls, low ceiling. The 1962 photo's coats-on-hooks win.
- `objects/commons-speed-graphic-press-camera-fbi-pd.jpg` (4016 x 5020, PD): black leatherette box, chrome struts,
  grey lens board lettered SPEED GRAPHIC / MODEL FP, lens in a shutter ring with its speed scale, a black cable
  release looping to the side, rangefinder windows on top, the drop bed, and the GRAFLEX flash gun: a brushed
  aluminium battery tube clamped on two brackets, with a big polished round reflector and the bulb socket in its
  centre. Cut out of the photo as is (`public/wilt-100/photodeveloping-speed-graphic.png`), not redrawn.
- `objects/commons-press-photographers-speed-graphic-1942-pd.jpg`: how it is held, up at the eye, flash gun up top.
- New: `objects/photodeveloping-flashbulbs-press25-gotanero-ccbysa4.jpg` (Commons "Flashbulbs - Sylvania Press 25,
  GE Photoflash 22, 75", Gotanero, CC BY-SA 4.0): the press bulb is a small clear glass globe packed with a tangle of
  fine shredded foil wire, on a brass base. A spent bulb goes milky and blackened inside. That is the bulb in the
  reflector.
- Commons has no Magic Marker of the era (searched): the marker is drawn as a plain chunky black felt marker.

## The real things and where each comes from

- the wall, hook rail, coats: the 100 photo's background + the 2001 locker room (block wall, bench);
- the blank print: photographic paper with a white border at the photo's real 2276 x 2879 proportion;
- the developer tray: an enamel darkroom tray (rolled rim, corner spout, ribbed floor), developer liquid;
- the counter: the reel's WILT flip counter (same flaps as `hershey`), whose 100 becomes ink;
- the sheet and its 100: the real marker strokes cut from the photo (de-rotated 13.5 degrees,
  `photodeveloping-ink-100.png`), revealed along traced centre lines, so the 100 we watch being written IS the 100;
- the camera: the real Speed Graphic cut out of the FBI photo; the bulb from the Press 25 photo;
- the payoff: the real AP photo, developing from white with the darkest tones first.

## Details that make it real

Fold crease and wavy top edge on the sheet; ink pooling darker where strokes overlap; the dot at the foot of the 1;
the coats swaying a hair; liquid ripples and the bulb's reflection wobbling in the tray; the print's white border;
drips falling off the lifted print; bamboo print tongs; the spent bulb gone milky; credit lines.

## Sound-off test

A blank print waits in a tray; a PR man's hand writes the real 100 on paper; a press camera flashes; the print in the
tray turns into the famous photo of Wilt holding that very sheet. A stranger reads: the iconic picture was improvised
on the spot with a sheet of paper and a camera that happened to be there.

Amber: only the flashbulb (a witness).
