# BallToAuction (`ball`, 68.12-76.07 s)

"And the ball? A 14 year old fan said he walked out with it, and it later sold at auction for 551,844 dollars."

## What I looked at

- `photos/commons-wilt-1959-pd.jpg` (crop at 3x): the era's Spalding leather ball under Wilt's hand. Thin dark seam
  channels, not wide stripes: one great circle running nearly level across the middle, a second curving down the
  side, the side seams bowing like brackets. Pebbled grain all over (visible even in a 1959 print), the light from
  upper left leaving a soft hot spot, a worn ink stamp (not copied) and a small round stamp by the valve. Matte
  leather, no gloss. In colour these balls were tan to brown leather; aged ones darker at the seams and where hands
  hold them.
- `photos/commons-hersheypark-arena-main-gate-2001-cooper-ccby3.jpg` (crop at 3x; the 960 px and original were
  refused by Commons, 429): the main gate. Cream stucco facade; a deep brown canopy fascia in stacked horizontal bands
  with a round medallion at its centre; above it the later "HERSHEYPARK ARENA" sign (cropped out: not the 1962
  name). Under the canopy a recessed entrance framed by two pilasters banded with horizontal grooves; two door bays
  with dark glass in thin metal frames; between them a white board (box office sign, later, drawn blank) and a lower
  placard; dark red mats on the concrete; a globe wall sconce on a bracket upper right; a downspout right of the
  entrance; evergreen shrubs in dark green planters; a squat trash bin.
- `photos/wilt-100-paper-photo-1962-original.jpg`, via the photo scene's last frame (print 460 x 582 at scale 1.38,
  centre 540,960, rotated -2.5 deg, 20 px white border): the hand-off picture I start from.
- No photo of the auctioned ball or the sale itself on the allowed hosts; the auction room is drawn from period
  auction-house furniture (walnut panelling, a turned gavel with a brass band on a round sounding block, white bidder
  paddles on turned handles, a lit plinth under a glass vitrine).

## The real thing and its details

- The ball: 3D sphere, eight panels (equator + meridian great circles, two small circles at x = +-0.7), seams as
  narrow near-black grooves with a lighter leather lip; pebble grain (turbulence) clipped to the ball; light from upper
  left; a darker hand-worn band; three scuffs and a worn stamp smudge that turn WITH the ball, so the same scuffs are
  recognisable in the auction case. It rotates as it rolls (real rolling: angle = distance / radius).
- The floor (inside): the arena's basketball floor laid over the ice: maple boards in perspective, grain, board ends
  staggered, a painted lane line, a dropped programme and a torn ticket stub (the fans flooded the floor a minute
  ago).
- The gate (outside, at night): redrawn from the Cooper photo minus the later sign. The right door pair stands open,
  warm light from the lobby spilling out onto the mats and the wet pavement; the sconce globe lit.
- The auction: walnut panelled wall with moulding, a spot from above, a black plinth, the glass vitrine with edge
  highlights and reflections, a typed lot card ("LOT", stamped 2000), the gavel and block, rows of chair backs in the
  foreground with bidder paddles rising. Amber: the ball's rim from "14" on (a witness kept it) and the winning
  paddle's number edge.

## Sound-off test

A ball drops, a kid's name appears on it, the ball rolls out of the arena's front door at night as if carried, years
roll to 2000, the same ball is under glass with paddles going up and a price climbing to $551,844 under a gavel.
A stranger reads: the 100-point ball left with a fan and sold decades later for a fortune.
