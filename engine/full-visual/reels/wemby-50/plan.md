## The viewer's wrong picture

"Fifty points means he just shot a lot." Points look like the whole story; the box score puts PTS in the biggest
column and the cost (FGA, FTA) in small columns nobody reads.

## The mechanism, at expert depth (what the viewer must leave with)

- A shot is not free: every field goal attempt ends one of the team's chances to score (a "shooting chance").
- A free throw trip is two shots for one chance, so a free throw counts as 0.44 of a chance (Basketball-Reference:
  `TSA = FGA + 0.44 x FTA`). 0.44 is a little under half because some trips are one shot (and-ones) or three.
  (Only "a little under half" is on screen; the and-one reason is in the voice's spirit, not a claim on screen.)
- Wemby: `29 + 0.44 x 6 = 31.64` chances. `50 / 31.64 = 1.58` points per chance.
- `TS% = PTS / (2 x TSA)`: halving puts a made two on every chance at 100%. His .790. League 2026-27 .575.
- Same 31.64 chances at .575: `2 x 31.64 x .575 = 36.4` points. Gap: 50 - 36.4 = 13.6, said "14".
- Where from: 10 of 12 at the rim, 5 of 11 threes (plus 2 of 2 from 4-9 ft and 3 of 4 midrange, on the chart but not
  spoken; they may be seen, never captioned). 31:51 on the floor. 114-91, won by 23.

Spoken "about 31.5" vs exact 31.64: the equation on screen shows the real `31.64` (what Basketball-Reference
computes); the caption keeps "31.5". Never put "31.5" as a computed result on stage. Counters land on 31.64, 1.58,
79.0 / .790, 57.5 / .575, 36 (36.4 rounds), 14 (13.6 rounds; written "+14" only where the voice says 14).

## Once for the reel

- **One world, three scales. The reel travels between them and comes back.**
  - DOCUMENT: the real records of the game. NBA.com's Spurs box score table, Basketball-Reference's basic and
    advanced rows and its 2026-27 league average row, and BBR's play-by-play lines for his 29 shots (the shot chart
    tooltips, real copy: "1st quarter, 10:46.0 remaining / Victor Wembanyama missed 3-pointer from 27 ft / San Antonio
    leads 3-2"). Where the cost hides.
  - FLOOR: the real NBA half court at true geometry (50 x 47 ft) with his 29 shots at their real places, seen top-down
    or from the stands. Where the cost is spent.
  - MAN: the real Wemby, from the Commons photos, in rounded cards with the credit top right, as in the first post.
    Who spent it.
- **Thread object: the half court with its 29 marks.** It is in the hook and the close, and is the hero of `where`.
  In between it is the ground the chances sit on, never the main picture twice in one framing: top-down full half
  court only in the hook, `where` and `close`; close at the rim with the shot clock in `cost`; the lane at MACRO in
  `freethrow`; the whole court from the side stands (the Toronto photo's height and tilt) in `attempts` and
  `average`. `answer` does not replay the court: its picture is the game in time (the play-by-play log).
- **The invisible thing: a chance, one shape, one colour, the whole reel.** A shooting chance is drawn where it was
  spent: a flat gold disc (#F2B33D, the gold data accent of the creator's reference reel) lying on the floor on the
  shot's spot, the size of the free throw circle's line width x 6 (about 1.6 ft across at court scale), a thin darker
  rim, a soft contact shadow, the boards' grain catching light through its edge. Not a poker chip, not a coin: it has
  no print, no stripes, no value written on it. 1 disc per field goal attempt; a free throw is a 0.44 wedge of a disc.
  A disc has depth (about 0.12 of its width) so it can stand, lift, stack and hold a fill: points fill it white, up to
  2 points (a made two) = full. Gold means "chance spent" and nothing else. Points are white. Makes and misses keep the
  Basketball-Reference chart convention (filled dot = made, x = missed), in white and grey. The word "chance" is
  written once, small with a hairline, on the first disc that is spent (`cost`).
- **Labels:** plain small grey type (#8B949E) with a hairline, 32 px minimum for anything that is evidence. Source
  lines bottom-left of the stage, 26 px grey: "NBA.com box score, Nov 21 2026", "Basketball-Reference glossary",
  "Basketball-Reference shot chart". Photo credits top right inside the photo card, 22 px, as in the first post.
- **No** pills, badges with dots, step cards, bar comparisons (two fills side by side are bars too: a comparison is
  two levels on the same disc), blue on stage, dashed lines, frosted panels, game footage (rights), invented app
  screens, "19 rebounds", "first in history", a hand, a ghost, a silhouette standing in for "an average scorer".
  Rebounds stay in the box row (18) as copy only, never pointed at.
- **Grounds:** all dark, #0D1117 with the house faint grid. Cards #161B22, hairline #30363D. The court floor is the
  one large warm surface: maple boards running baseline to half court (2.25 in boards, scaled), satin sheen, a slow
  light sweep, white 2 in lines; lane painted in Spurs black with a silver edge (Spurs colours #000000, #C4CED4).
  The court sits as a lit slab on the navy, never edge to edge bare wood with no ground around it.
- **One big move (body):** in `attempts`, on "up", every gold disc on the court lifts off its shot spot at once,
  flies in a curved stream and stacks into one column beside the court (29 whole discs, then the 6 free throw wedges
  that fuse into 2.64), heavy, with a settle. The reel never does anything that loud again.
- **The one turn:** `average`, the same discs on the same spots filled at the league rate: the only time the points
  are not his. Shown on the discs themselves, never by a figure standing in for a player.
- **Last shot = first shot, resolved:** `close` is the hook's frame (50 big, 29 small), but now the 29 is made of
  discs and the court lives under it.
