# Four reels to learn from

The biggest reels this format has made, as reading material in this skill's `examples/` folder
(`.agents/skills/full-visual/examples/<slug>/`). Each holds:

- `script.txt` (the words), `script.v3.txt` (the same words with their voice tags), `caption.txt` (the caption and
  its scenes), `reel.json` (the voice and theme), `facts.md` where it was written;
- `plan.md`, the visual plan the builders worked from;
- `stills/`, eight frames from the finished reel (the file name is the second it was taken at): the level to reach;
- `code/`, the code of two of its scenes, the hook and one hero scene, with their helper files.

They are not reels you can render here: their voices, footage and other scenes stay with their creator. Read the
plan beside the stills, then the code: that is how a sentence became a picture.

Copy their thinking and their care, never their pictures or their words. Each plan was written for the creator who
built the format: "his" in a plan means that creator, and a plan's notes on a presenter or on a file that is not
here do not apply.

## touchscreen: why your screen ignores a pen

The everyday thing, the impossible-looking feat, and one hidden mechanism. "Your phone screen reacts to the lightest
brush of your finger and ignores a pen pressed as hard as you can. So what is it actually feeling? It isn't really
feeling pressure, it's measuring the electricity in your body."

- One world, three scales: the screen cut open side-on (glass, touch layer, OLED, frame, read off teardown photos),
  the wire grid as the phone sees it, a gloved hand answering a call in the snow.
- The invisible (the charge at each crossing) has one look for the whole reel, so the eye follows it.
- Each sentence changes one real thing; "that's also why" pays off rain and touchscreen gloves; the last shot
  resolves the first.
- Code: `HookX1*` (the hook: finger against pen on the section) and `WireGrid*` (the wire grid, built and read).

## iphone-keyboard: the keys you touch are not the keys you see

Same shape, a different wrong picture: "Every key on your iPhone keyboard is smaller than your thumb and you still
hit the right letter almost every time."

- One thread object, the real iOS keyboard redrawn at true geometry, and one invisible thing under it: the hit
  regions, amber, swelling and squeezing as the next letter becomes likely.
- A real source on screen (the engineer's book, the patent sheet), real numbers (44 pixels, 320 wide).
- Code: `HookX1*` (the hook) and `KbZoneGrow*` (the zone around the next letter growing under the thumb).

## ps5-on-pc: PS5 games booting on a PC

News said plainly, then the mechanism. The hook the format holds as its bar: "Sony wants you to stop owning your
games. So the community rebuilt the PS5 in open source." (`script.txt` holds the body with its first hook, "GTA 5,
Spider-Man, Silent Hill 2. Their PS5 versions already boot on a PC, with a free emulator called KytyPS5.")

- Real material everywhere: the real games, real gameplay footage, the real function names a game calls, the real
  GPU instructions, the real repositories.
- One wiring board carried through three scenes, its sockets filled one by one as the explanation climbs.
- Code: `HookVC*` (the console decomposed, each noun a real thing on its word, the code and the people who rebuilt
  it around it) and `KytyLive*` (the emulator answering the game's calls, live).

## pewdiepie-abliteration: how an AI is made to never say no

A story people already heard, explained at depth. "How did PewDiePie make an uncensored AI that never says no?"

- One picture held all the way: a 3D map where every request is a point. It is turned, measured, pressed flat and
  damaged, so a hard idea (a direction in a model's space) becomes something you watch happen.
- The catch and its number (math scores dropping), a free tool, and a check the viewer can run.
- Code: `map.ts` and `MapSpace.tsx` (one camera, real projection, one world), `HookX2*` (a hook built in that
  world) and `Flatten*` (the map pressed flat).

## What they share

The thing named in the first words, a feat or a story the viewer already knows, the answer given away early, one lived
scene, a chain of real events each caused by the last, a second layer, a close that echoes the first sentence, and
something to save or send. On screen: real things drawn with care, one world, the invisible made visible, something
new on every key word, and a first second that already says the idea with the sound off.
