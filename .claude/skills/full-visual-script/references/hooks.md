# Hooks that stopped the scroll

The first one or two sentences, as spoken. What these share: the thing named at once, one idea, absolute and
concrete, true, and a picture you could draw word by word. Most carry a contradiction; not all of them do. Write
three or four of different kinds, then pick.

## The impossible feat

A feat the viewer has lived, with the obvious explanation taken away. Then their question, short.

> Your phone screen reacts to the lightest brush of your finger and ignores a pen pressed as hard as you can.
> So what is it actually feeling?

> Every key on your iPhone keyboard is smaller than your thumb and you still hit the right letter almost every time.
> So how is that possible?

> AirPods can make a plane engine disappear and they are not blocking the sound.
> So where does the noise go?

Variations on the same move:

- **Triple negation:** "[thing] has no A, no B and no C, and still [does it]." ("A dead iPhone has no screen, no
  internet and no GPS, and Find My can still put it on a map.")
- **Impossible condition:** "[thing] can [do it] in [the condition that should break it]."
- **Secret identity:** "Your [thing] is secretly [doing something you never suspected]." ("Your AirPods make
  silence by playing more sound.")
- **The everyday change:** "Your old laptop charger was a brick, and the one that replaced it fits in your pocket and
  charges faster."

## The belief turned over

Says the viewer's wrong picture out loud, then breaks it in the same breath.

> Your phone screen can't feel your finger.

> Your iPhone keyboard changes size while you type and you have never seen it.

## The news, said big and plain

The payoff first, with names people know, no hedging, no list pauses.

> GTA 5, Spider-Man, Silent Hill 2. Their PS5 versions already boot on a PC, with a free emulator called KytyPS5.

> You can now play your PS5 games directly on your PC.

## The stakes, a villain

A side people can feel, then what happened. The hook the format holds as its bar is this one, because each noun is a
real thing you can draw and each verb is done to it:

> Sony wants you to stop owning your games. So the community rebuilt the PS5 in open source.

## The question people already have

A story everyone heard, asked as the question nobody can answer. Plain, curious, the last word falling.

> How did PewDiePie make an uncensored AI that never says no?

> How do Chinese AI companies copy Anthropic models?

> How did 700 OpenAI agents end up hacking Hugging Face?

## What lost

- A soft question where a flat statement was true ("Have you ever wondered...").
- Two ideas in one hook, or a list read with pauses ("GTA, ... Spider-Man, ...").
- A spec as the first fact ("one chip stays awake for up to 5 more hours" reads as a datasheet).
- Jargon the viewer has never heard ("abliterated") or a detail number nobody can picture.
- A claim that is not quite true ("any video game"). The comments find it.

## How to pick

Pick the one a stranger understands instantly, that can be drawn as one hero decoded word by word, and that is
completely true. Say why in one line. The creator decides.
