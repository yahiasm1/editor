# The bar

The house bar, in the words of the creator who built this format and steered every reel in `references/examples.md`.
A creator who saved their own look, voice or words (`workspace/library/full-visual.preferences.md`) keeps this bar for
the picture and the motion, and their own for the rest.

## What the viewer should feel

That they saw inside something. The best reels felt like a cross-section of a thing the viewer holds every day, or a
map of a story they had already heard, explored from several sides until it clicked. One subject, many angles, every
sentence with its own picture, real things drawn with care, the invisible part made visible, and the first second
already saying the idea with the sound off.

## The picture

- **Real, detailed, attentive.** The note given most often after a first try: "it can be more real, with much more
  details, much more attention to it. It should really go in depth." A real thing has parts, materials, labels,
  wear, light and scale. Draw those, from real photos, teardowns and screens. Flat boxes and generic shapes read as
  coded ("it looks vibe coded", "too few parts", "reads as AI").
- **A new real thing for every sentence group.** An interface, a document, a machine opened up. A thread object can
  stay across the reel; it is never the main picture twice.
- **Show the mechanism working.** An abstract idea is drawn doing its job, step by step on the words, with real
  numbers. Never blur, frost or a vague panel: "it's blurry, we see no animation, nothing moving".
- **Cut inside.** When the mechanism is inside, show the inside: the screen as a side-on section, the shell turning
  see-through, the layers lifting out like a teardown photo. A product shot alone explains nothing.
- **One world, three scales.** Inside the object, the object in the hand, the place around it. Travel between them
  and come back, so the viewer never loses where they are.
- **The invisible gets one shape and one colour for the whole reel** (the charge, the hit region, the refusal
  direction). Everything else keeps its real colours, so the accent is what pulls the eye.
- **Fill the stage.** Foreground, subject, depth behind, secondary motion. Never one small object on an empty ground.
  Mass and material: thousands of small real things forming one structure, detail at several scales, light that
  moves across surfaces.
- **Every pattern says what it is.** A chunk, a spike, a cell: the word it stands for is written on it, small, with a
  hairline. An unlabelled pattern reads as decoration.
- **One focus at a time.** Big and centred first, then it moves aside as the rest arrives. Clear beats complete.
- **The last shot is the first shot, resolved.**

## The motion

- Something meaningful moves on nearly every word, and the move is the mechanism working, not decoration.
- Every change belongs to a word: an event lands on it, a build ends on it. Followable, then a short hold.
- The first ten seconds carry the most motion. Nothing sits still for more than about a second.
- A held picture keeps living: a slow push in, light travelling, small parts working.
- Handovers are part of the craft: checked frame by frame, no snap, no jump, no ghost copy of a shared object.

## The hook (the first three to four seconds)

The most important part of the reel, and the reason five are built.

- **The sentence decoded.** One hero, alone, each noun entering as a real thing on its word, each verb done
  physically to the hero, one continuous transformation, readable with the sound off. The bar is
  `examples/ps5-on-pc/` (hook C, its code in `code/HookVC*`): the console decomposed, the open-source code and the people who rebuilt it around it.
- **Frame 0 is full and already moving.** Never an empty ground, a title or one object waiting. The mechanism plays
  once, small and fast, inside the first second; something new every 0.2 to 0.4 s after that.
- **One big physical move with weight** on the key word: depth, inertia, real material. Then the payoff holds.
- Losers: a UI story, a stack of cards, camera acrobatics instead of the idea, anything that looks coded.

## Never

These read as AI, and each one was rejected on a real reel:

- pills and badges with a dot ("WHILE YOU PLAY"), coloured status labels, step-number cards, a headline that repeats
  the caption, a pinned stepper;
- rows of coloured bars for a comparison or a score (draw the real artifact);
- blue type, numbers or lines on the stage (the caption marker is the engine's and stays);
- fake games, fake apps, fake documents when the real one exists ("take actual videos");
- a 3D set that replaces the real thing, mannequins, AI imagery that does not read photoreal;
- a terminal at the bottom, dashed connector lines, frosted blur as content, four cards at once;
- splitting a product in two with a cleaver cut, a hero mechanism dimmed while the voice moves on.

## The words

The voice is alive and fast, with emotion that follows each sentence (`references/voice.md`). Every claim on screen
is in the reel's `facts.md`. No em dashes anywhere on screen.
